How to read this — and how the math works

Why a single date is a bad commitment. Adding up everyone's "likely" estimate produces a plan that only works if nothing runs long. Because delays compound and parallel work waits on its slowest branch, that sum typically lands somewhere near a 20–40% chance of being met. That is the number most slipped programs were quietly built on.

P50 / P80 / P90. P80 means four runs in five finished by that date. The gap between P50 and P80 is the size of the risk you are carrying — a wide gap means your plan's outcome depends heavily on luck, and needs either buffer or de-risking, not optimism.

The simulation. Each run draws a duration for every task from its three-point estimate, takes the longest task in each phase, and sums the phases. Repeating that tens of thousands of times builds the distribution of possible finish dates. PERT weights the likely case four times as heavily as the extremes (the standard project-estimating choice). Triangular spreads probability more evenly, which is the safer pick when your "likely" figure is really a guess.

Sensitivity. Each task's sampled duration is correlated against the total across all runs. A high score means that task's uncertainty is what decides your date. Tasks buried inside a parallel phase behind a much longer sibling will score near zero — they are not on your risk path, and shortening them buys you nothing.

Estimating honestly. Best case should be genuinely unobstructed, not sandbagged. Worst case should be a bad-but-real week, not a catastrophe. If a task's worst case is more than roughly three times its best case, it is too big — split it.

Limits worth knowing. Task durations are drawn independently, so a shared root cause (one vendor, one sick engineer, one flaky environment) that hits several tasks at once is not modelled — treat the P80 as a floor in that situation. Scope changes, unknown work and cross-phase dependency chains beyond the phase grouping are also out of scope.

Privacy. Everything runs locally in your browser. No data leaves the page, there is no analytics, no network request and no external script. Your task list is kept in this browser's local storage only, and "Clear all" removes it.
